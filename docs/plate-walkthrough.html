A cooking companion

Plate

Swipe through recipe ideas, keep track of what is in your fridge, and cook with an assistant that answers questions out loud while your hands are busy.

Expo · React Native Node · MongoDB Gemini ElevenLabs Auth0

The idea

Dinner, from pantry to plate

Four tabs carry the whole app. Everything else — recipe details, cooking mode, chat, fridge, preferences — opens on top of them.

Discover

A swipe deck of ideas ranked against your taste, your diet, your clock, and what is already in the fridge.

Saved

Every recipe you swiped right on, searchable by name or ingredient and filterable by cuisine.

Cart

A shopping list built from what a recipe is missing — then move the checked items straight into your fridge.

You

Your kitchen: fridge, taste and diet, swipe history, past conversations, and settings.

How it is put together

The app holds no keys

Every AI call and every database read or write goes through the API. Gemini, ElevenLabs, MongoDB, and Unsplash credentials live only in server/.env, and the app authenticates with an Auth0 access token.

React Native app

Getting in

Plate never stores a password

Auth0 owns identity over OAuth with PKCE. The API derives the user from the verified token rather than a client-supplied id, so one account can never read another's fridge or recipes.

  • Email sign-in plus Apple and Google, at least 8 characters
  • Create account, forgot password, reset — the reset email comes from Auth0
  • First sign-in bootstraps the account — user, preferences, settings, and an empty cart are created in one go

Personalisation

The deck is scored, not shuffled

Plate reads your last 300 swipes to build a taste profile — a right swipe counts +1.0, a left swipe −0.6 — then ranks a pool of up to 200 unseen recipes against it. Anything that fails your allergy or diet rules is dropped before scoring even starts.

  • Already-seen and already-saved recipes are excluded, so the deck never repeats itself
  • If the pool runs dry, Gemini writes new cards — prompted with the dishes you liked, the ones you passed on, and an exclusion list
“That is everything for now — Plate has shown you every match for these filters. Widen them or ask for something specific.” Discover, empty state

Swaps and adjustments

Change the recipe, keep the guardrails

Tap any ingredient for up to three substitutions. Each candidate is run back through the same allergy and diet check as a fresh recipe, and unsafe ones are dropped before you ever see them.

  • “Suggestions respect your allergies and diet.” When nothing safe exists it says “No safe swap found.”
  • “Adjust” rescales servings from 1 to 12, or takes a plain-English instruction
  • An adjusted recipe is a new recipe — re-validated on the server, so an edit cannot smuggle an allergen back in

  • Every result is checked before it is shown, not after you have started cooking

Cooking mode

One step at a time, hands free

Cooking mode keeps the screen awake and shows a single step at a time, with the next one previewed underneath. Your position is written to MongoDB, so closing the app and coming back resumes exactly where you were.

  • Timers appear only when the step names a duration. A range like “8–10 minutes” uses the longer end
  • The mic answers in place — the reply lands in a “Plate says” card with “Open full conversation”
  • Ingredients stay one tap away, grouped into in fridge, still needed, and optional
“Time is up. Check the pan before moving on.” … then, at the end: “Nice work. This one is marked as cooked.” Cooking mode

Talking to Plate

An assistant that knows which step you are on

Chat threads are scoped: one for a recipe, one for a cooking session, or a general thread. Whichever you open, Plate is given your allergies, your fridge, the recipe, and the step you are standing on — plus the last twelve messages.

  • Speak or type. ElevenLabs transcribes the audio, then reads the answer back if you want
  • Send a photo when it is easier to show than describe — “Plate reads it to answer, and keeps only its description”
  • Threads are titled automatically in three to five words, and old conversations stay browsable
“Hey Plate” does not listen in the background. The app says so verbatim: always-on wake-word detection needs a native engine this build does not include. The microphone button is the real trigger.

Taste and diet

Allergies are limits. Dislikes are opinions.

The preferences screen saves as you tap. It separates the things Plate must obey from the things it should merely lean away from — and says which is which, in the app.

On allergies: “Plate rejects any recipe containing these, including hidden sources such as soy sauce for gluten or fish sauce for fish. Always double-check labels yourself.”
  • Dislikes: “Plate avoids these but will not refuse a recipe over them.”
  • Nutrition goals: “Used to steer recipes, not to police them.”
  • Also here: skill, effort, a time ceiling from 15 to 120 minutes, default servings, meal types, budget, and metric or imperial

Allergies — 12 enforced allergens

Peanuts Tree nuts Dairy Eggs Gluten Shellfish Fish Soy Sesame Mustard Celery Sulfites

Diets — 13 are hard rules, the rest only steer

Vegan Vegetarian Pescatarian Halal Kosher Gluten-free Dairy-free Keto Paleo Mediterranean High-protein
Advisory diets steer generation but never block a recipe. Keto, paleo, low-carb, whole30, mediterranean, low-sodium, high-protein, and diabetic-friendly sit in that group.

Safety

Allergies are enforced twice

Asking a model nicely is not enforcement. Plate sends your allergies as hard constraints, and then re-reads every recipe it gets back — title, description, each ingredient name and note, and every instruction — before you are allowed to see it.

Saved · Swipe history

Nothing you swiped is thrown away

  • “Search saved recipes or ingredients” — the search reads the ingredient list, not just the title
  • Unsaving deletes the matching swipe too, so the recipe is free to come back around in the deck
  • Tab badges are real counts from MongoDB, capped at “99+”, hidden at zero

The look

One token file, two schemes

Every colour, radius, and type size lives in src/constants/theme.ts. Components read from useTheme() and never hardcode a hex value, and TypeScript requires every light token to exist in dark — so the two schemes cannot drift apart.

  • Terracotta on warm cream. #e85d3f on #fff8ef, with herb and spice washes behind every screen
  • Follows the phone by default; Settings can pin light or dark
  • A small deliberate ramp — six spacing steps, six radii, nine type sizes, two elevations. Hierarchy stays readable because there is not much to choose from

What Plate refuses to fake

The honest defaults

These are deliberate. Where the app cannot know something, it says so instead of filling the gap with something plausible.

A swipe is not a swipe until it is stored

The card leaves the deck after MongoDB confirms the write. A failure springs it back and tells you.

No stock photo of a different meal

A photo appears only when the backend found a real one for that dish. Otherwise Plate draws its own cover.

“Hey Plate” is not listening

Wake-word detection is reported unsupported and the app repeats it verbatim. The mic button is the trigger.

No invented prices or delivery times

No grocery provider ships with Plate, so the cart is a list and ordering is simply unavailable.

Nutrition is labelled an estimate

Fields the model did not provide are left out rather than guessed at.

Photos and audio are not kept

Images and recordings are analysed in the request and discarded; only the derived description is stored.
